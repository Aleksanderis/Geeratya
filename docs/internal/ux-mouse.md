# UX: mouse

*Internal design note for maintainers and agents. Not needed to use or contribute to Geeratya.*

Part of the UX spec; start at [ux.md](ux.md). Model: the mouse note of the owner's other terminal client (a table of gesture to action id), rewritten for Geeratya.

## Principle

Mouse input is optional and never the only way: everything doable by mouse is doable by keyboard and the reverse. A gesture on an area becomes the **action id** a key would produce, and the same reducer handler runs it (the `ActionRequested` command, which a wheel notch repeats 3 times like `3j`). There is no second code path for the mouse; the only commands of its own are `PointerPlaced` (a click places the cursor, see below) and `TabActivated`, and both drop a half-typed chord and count like an action does.

## Layers

1. The terminal driver reports SGR mouse events (Terminal.Gui enables them; no setting is needed).
2. `MouseFlagTranslator` (Tui, `Input/`) turns the toolkit's flags into a `MouseGesture` (click, double-click, right-click, middle-click, Ctrl-click, wheel up or down). A pointer move with no button held is not a gesture: `IsHover` turns it into `TabHovered`, which only makes the hovered tab chip show its close `x` (no action id, no state beyond the highlight; moves outside the tab row are ignored). Presses, releases, other buttons and a sideways wheel are ignored.
3. `MouseHitTester` (Core, pure) turns a cell and a `MouseContext` (size, header rows, overlay open, issue open, the cells of each tab, of the `+` button, of the active tab's `x` and of the jump chip, and whether the banner shows a line) into a `MouseTarget`: the tab bar row, the banner row, the body (lists or detail) and the status line.
4. `DefaultMouseMap` (Core) gives the row for the target and gesture; `MouseResolver` makes the commands from it (a list: an `ActionRequested`, or a `PointerPlaced` that carries the action to run after placing).

While an overlay, prompt, picker, form, editor or help is open, only the tab bar and banner rows count, as the `Backdrop` (a click there closes the overlay like `Esc`), and the clickable rows the overlay reports. The renderer places the overlay and hands the hit test its row rectangles (`OverlayRegion`, in `StyledFrame.Regions`), so the hit test never guesses the layout; every overlay also reports its whole box (`OverlayBox`, rows on top of it): a click inside the box that hits no row does nothing, and a click on the body outside the box is the Backdrop and closes it like `Esc` (#525; the status bar is not backdrop, except while a confirm is open). The issue detail is drawn in the wide overlay (`DetailOverlayGeometry`, `docs/internal/ux-screens.md` "Where it is drawn"); it is no mode, so the tab bar stays clickable, and a click on the body outside its box closes the whole issue stack (`IssueDetailCloseRequested`; `Esc` pops one issue). Below 100 columns the overlay fills the body and there is no backdrop. The Confirm dialog reports `ConfirmBox` the same way and, because the dialog is the whole question, also counts the status line as backdrop: a click away from it is a no. A click on a row resolves to `OverlayRowClicked` (the row is chosen, then the key's handler runs); only the row targets resolve that way, and a click on a form field while it saves or asks to discard does nothing, as its keys do nothing. The wheel is the exception for the Help overlay, the Messages overlay and the context menus (#570): it acts on the open one wherever the pointer is on the screen (`MouseContext.WheelTarget`, no rectangle needed), 3 lines or rows per notch; any other overlay ignores it, except the palette and Settings rows above.

## Parts

The table is delivered in parts of issue #200: **A** the input layer and this table (wheel, status line, backdrop; the other rows exist in the table but are not wired yet: `MouseResolver.WiredParts`), **B** view interaction (select a row, card, column or line by click, double-click to open, right-click, tab and banner clicks), **O** overlays (this slice of the issue #200 part C: the rows of the source picker, jump prompt, profile list, field forms and suggestion popups; the editor text itself takes no clicks), **M** menu and drag (this slice of part C: the right-click menu of the issue under the pointer, `<space>a` as its key, drag of a card to a neighbouring column; the `+` tab button and the hidden-tab and neighbour-column marks belong to part B and are wired), **C** the rest of the writes (status badge, assignee, `+` buttons, comments, `Ctrl`-click rank). **V** the menu of the tab and the view (this slice of part C: right-click on a tab or the status line, `<space>A` as its key; `Esc` closes it). A row moves to the wired set when its part lands.

## Placing the cursor

A plain click on a list, a board or an opened issue has no action id: it does what motions do for a key and places the cursor, through the `PointerPlaced` command (the motions are listed under "Without a path"). On a list it selects the row under the pointer; on the columns layout the card under it, or the lane of a clicked chip of the lane strip (#874, the first body row while a grouping is on: that lane's columns are shown and the cursor goes to its first card; the other rows of the board are one row lower then, and no action of a double-click or right-click runs on a chip), or the column of a clicked column title in the top edge (that column keeps its own card; in a window of one column the neighbour names of the header, `‹ Name n` and `Name n ›`, select that neighbour); in an opened issue the line, or the pane when the click is on a frame edge (see "Detail targets"; rows past the end do nothing; the view does not scroll). Rows without an issue, the top edge with the view title (in the columns layout it is the column header row, as above) and the area under the cards do nothing. Double-click and right-click place the cursor first, then run their action, so they act on the issue under the pointer (a right-click opens the menu of that issue: `ContextMenu` rows listed in the table, one `Actions` box; `j`/`k`, `Enter` and `Esc` work in it like in the pickers, and every item runs the handler of its key, so delete and unlink still ask first); on empty space (nothing placed) the action does not run. Cells come from the same measures the renderer draws with (`BoardHits`, `TabBar.Areas`), so a layout change moves the click areas with it.

## Detail targets

The opened issue is drawn in the wide overlay of panes (`docs/internal/ux-screens.md`, "Panes, focus and zoom"). Its clicks are placing clicks (the `PointerPlaced` command, no action id of their own: the detail hit test `DetailPointer` finds the strip chip, row or frame under the cell with the measures the renderer draws with), and a plain click also runs `item.open` on the row it placed the cursor on where that row has a natural "go" (the same handler as `Enter`). Nothing is a mouse-only path: every row below is a key (`Ctrl-h/j/k/l`, `j`/`k`, `Enter`, `Ctrl-h`/`Ctrl-l` in the zoom, `Esc`).

| Where | Gesture | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A pane's rows | Click | Focuses the pane and puts the cursor on the row (a comment's body rows select that comment) |
| A pane's top or bottom edge | Click | Focuses that pane (the first or last row of it); the pane does not fold (#702); no double-click or right-click action follows |
| A pane | Wheel | The pane under the pointer takes the focus when the cursor is in another one, then scrolls 3 lines (inside the pane: Comments, the cut Description, a zoomed pane); over the backdrop it scrolls the focused pane |
| Details sidebar | Click | Focuses the sidebar and the field under the pointer; on the empty space or the frame it focuses the sidebar |
| A Details field's label | Click | Focuses the field only |
| A Details field's value or its `▾` | Click | Focuses the field and opens its dropdown (editable fields only: those that show `▾`; status opens the `Move to` picker) |
| A dropdown row | Click | Accepts it, same as `Enter` on it (the write follows the normal path) |
| Outside the dropdown | Click | Closes it, same as `Esc` (the issue stays open); nothing else is clicked |
| Sub-tasks, Child issues, Linked issues row | Click | Opens that issue in the stack, same as `Enter` |

Removed with the old layout (#702, #714): the fold by a pane's title edge and the clickable field rows of the old header. ## Without a path

`TabHovered` (a pointer move over a tab chip from 80 columns, with no button held) is not an action: it only makes the chip show its close `x`, and `TabClosed` is the close behind both the `x` and the middle click. Actions with no row of their own, each with the reason (a test checks this list against `DefaultMouseMap.Exclusions`): the motions `nav.left`, `nav.right`, `nav.top`, `nav.bottom`, `nav.page-down`, `nav.page-up`, `nav.first-column` and `nav.last-column` (the pointer places the cursor and the wheel scrolls), `nav.up` and `nav.down` (the wheel rows), `detail.section-previous` (a click on a section title places the cursor; the lanes wrap, so `[[` has no click of its own), `tab.go-to.1` to `tab.go-to.9` (a click on tab N), `tab.last-active` (a click on any tab reaches it directly), `help.page-previous` (a click on the section name), `help.half-page-down`, `help.half-page-up`, `help.top` and `help.bottom` (the wheel rows scroll the help by lines; the jumps are keys), `help.filter` (the filter is typed text; the section names are the mouse path), `picker.open-new-tab` (sprint switcher only: a click on a row opens it like `Enter`; the key adds the new tab), `view.cycle-command-level` and `nav.row-jump` (their palette rows: click the status line, then the row), and `pane.focus-left`, `pane.focus-down`, `pane.focus-up`, `pane.focus-right` (a click inside a pane focuses it) and `pane.zoom` (the zoom chip of a pane): the click rows come with the pane screens (#702, #703); `detail.edit` (`i`: a double click on the title or Description pane edits it, through `item.open`, the same handler as `Enter`).

## Checks and terminal selection

## Writes

Destructive actions reached by mouse (context menu `issue.delete`, `comment.delete`, `issue.unlink`, drags that change status or rank) go through the same Confirm mode as their keys and say what will change; a drag never writes on release without the same checks as its key.

## Drag

The terminal reports the press and the release of the left button; `MouseDragTracker` pairs them into a drag when the release is on another cell (the click event the toolkit may send afterwards is dropped, so a drop never also selects). `MouseResolver.ResolveDrag` accepts it on the body only (never while an overlay is open). In the list and backlog layouts the held button is live (#571): the tracker reports each motion of the held button (`TrackMotion`, the `LeftButtonPressed` plus `PositionReport` events), `MouseResolver.ResolveDragMove` turns it into `RowsDragging`, whose first move starts Visual mode on the row of the press and whose later moves put the cursor on the row under the pointer, so the range follows while the button is down. While the pointer rests outside the body (above the first row or below the last, even past the terminal edge) `DragAutoScroller` (a timer in the shell, 80 ms, none in the reducer) raises a step with `DragAutoScroll.StepFor` rows and the reducer moves the cursor with `RowsDragScrolled` (the view scrolls with it); the speed is 1 row just outside, 1 more per 2 rows of distance, at most 6, and nothing moves at either end of the list. The release (`RowsDragged`, marked live) only moves the cursor to the release row when the pointer is on a row, keeps the range when it is outside the body (no write happens). On a sectioned Scrum backlog (#604) the range never spans sections: a pointer over another section's header, goal, note or row holds the cursor on the first or last row of the pressed row's section (keys `v` plus motions can still span sections; a sprint move of such a range is the same confirmed `<space>sm`), and the auto-scroll (same speed table) scrolls the window by display lines, so a long section or a far section comes into view, with the cursor on the row at the window edge clamped to the pressed section; the release on another section's line, also after a scroll (the pressed row is then the Visual anchor, not the press cell), asks to move the range, the marks or the row, as above; on the columns layout nothing is live. There it becomes `CardDragged`, which the reducer treats as `Alt-l` or `Alt-h` for the card under the press: the card is selected, then the same transition rules run (one matching transition is sent at once, otherwise the picker or form opens and confirms). Only a drop on the next or previous column acts; two or more columns away says `Drop the card on the next or previous column.` and writes nothing, and a drop on the same column, outside the columns, or from a column header does nothing. On a Scrum backlog, a release inside another section (header, goal, note or row line) is not a selection but a sprint move (`SprintMoveActions.Dropped`): the pressed row, or the marks or Visual range it belongs to, go to that sprint or the backlog after the Confirm dialog, which a drag always shows (a drop on a closed sprint is impossible: closed sprints have no section). A drag that ends in the same section, or in a view without sections, still selects rows. While the sprint list has not loaded the drop says so and does nothing. A window of one column (narrow terminals) has no drop target: use `Alt-l` / `Alt-h`. Checked in a real terminal session (tmux, SGR, fake Jira, #364): the toolkit delivers `LeftButtonPressed` at the press, the motion of the held button as `LeftButtonPressed` plus `PositionReport` (the tracker does not take it for a new press; in the list layouts it is the live range), `LeftButtonReleased` at the release cell and then a `LeftButtonClicked` at the release cell (swallowed). A drop sends a single matching transition at once, like the key, with no extra confirm (same as `Alt-l`, see #364; Jira has no undo for either).
